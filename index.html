A Survey of Multi-Robot Navigation in Social Mini-Games

Rohan Chandra1, Shubham Singh2, Wenhao Luo3, Katia Sycara4
1University of Virginia, 2University of Texas at Austin, 3University of Illinois Chicago, 4Carnegie Mellon University
Paper arXiv Video Code

Abstract

The ``Last Mile Challenge'' has long been considered an important, yet unsolved, challenge for autonomous vehicles, public service robots, and delivery robots. A central issue in this challenge is the ability of robots to navigate constrained and cluttered environments (e.g., doorways, hallways, corridor intersections), often while competing for space with other robots and humans. We refer to these environments as ``Social Mini-Games'' (SMGs). SMGs are tightly coupled, high-agency interactions that arise within general multi-robot navigation (MRN) scenarios. They are identified through certain distinct characteristics and require specialized metrics to evaluate them. Traditional navigation approaches designed for MRN do not perform well in SMGs, which has led to focused research on dedicated SMG solvers (navigation methods specialized to navigate in SMGs), which has flourished in recent years. However, publications on SMG navigation research make different assumptions (on centralized versus decentralized, observability, communication, cooperation, etc.), and have different objective functions (safety versus liveness). These assumptions and objectives are sometimes implicitly assumed or described informally. This makes it difficult to establish appropriate baselines for comparison in research papers, as well as making it difficult for practitioners to find the papers relevant to their concrete application. Such ad-hoc representation of the field also presents a barrier to new researchers wanting to start research in this area. SMG navigation research requires its own taxonomy, definitions, and evaluation protocols to guide effective research moving forward. This survey is the first to catalog SMG solvers using a well-defined and unified taxonomy and to classify existing methods accordingly. It also discusses the essential properties of SMG solvers, defines what SMGs are and how they appear in practice, outlines how to evaluate SMG solvers, and highlights the differences between SMG solvers and general navigation systems. The survey concludes with an overview of future directions and open challenges in the field.

What are Social Mini-Games?

Social Mini-Games (SMGs)
  • High agency — small actions significantly impact others
  • Constrained environments (doorways, hallways, intersections)
  • Typically 2–4 tightly coupled agents
  • Deadlocks, collisions, and symmetry are core challenges
  • Require specialized solvers and SMG-aware metrics
General MRN
  • Low to medium agency — agents mostly independent
  • Open environments with ample space
  • Decoupled trajectories most of the time
  • Standard collision avoidance usually suffices
  • Traditional metrics (path length, time) are adequate

An SMG arises when preferred trajectories of two or more agents must conflict within a time interval — forcing coordination.

Key Characteristics of SMGs

  • 1Mutual Occupancy Contention — Agent convex hulls persistently intersect across individual optimal trajectories.
  • 2Capacity-one Resource Contention — Two or more agents contend for a shared, capacity-one resource (doorway, merge lane).
  • 3Crossing Flows — Orthogonal or oblique approach trajectories require right-of-way or turn-taking.
  • 4Occlusion / Limited Observability — Beliefs about others' motion/goals materially shift best responses (e.g., blind corners).
  • 5Conflicting Objectives — Heterogeneous costs (speed vs. smoothness, priority agent vs. regular agent) create trade-offs.

9 Canonical SMG Scenarios

Doorway
Bottleneck navigation through tight spaces; tests prioritization and congestion handling.
Intersection
Agents from different directions meet; tests complex multi-agent interaction handling.
Hallway
Two-way corridor traffic; tests overtaking, yielding, and head-on deadlock prevention.
L-Corner
Sharp turn in an L-shaped environment; limited visibility requires strong deadlock avoidance.
Blind Corner
Limited visibility and sudden encounters; tests anticipation and real-time reaction.
Crowded Traffic
Many dynamic agents moving randomly; tests navigation through dense, busy environments.
Parallel Traffic
Agents moving in parallel paths; tests side-by-side deadlock avoidance and speed handling.
Perpendicular Traffic
Crossing paths perpendicularly; tests right-of-way management at intersections.
Circular Traffic
Circular flow patterns; tests merging, navigating through, and exiting without deadlocks.

Algorithm Paradigms

SMG solvers are categorized into four broad paradigms, each governing how agents in a coupling set generate strategies.

🤖
MARL
Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning. Agents learn policies through interaction; models strategic coupling via stochastic games. Handles non-stationarity from other learning agents.
🗺
MAPF
Multi-Agent Path Finding. Computes conflict-free paths on graphs. Discrete, centralized, fully cooperative. Best as a joint-planning subroutine for resolving bottleneck deadlocks.
🛠
Optimization
Constrained optimal control (MPC/CBFs). Minimizes cost subject to dynamics and collision constraints. Exposes SMG structure via explicit constraints and multi-objective costs.
💡
Others
Heuristics, evolutionary algorithms, game theory, and hybrid approaches. Adaptable and well-suited for warehousing, logistics, and large-scale systems.

Representative SMG Solvers

CADRL

Deep RL collision avoidance with LSTM for varying agent counts

IMPC-DR

MPC with buffered Voronoi cells for deadlock prevention

ORCA-MAPF

Hybrid ORCA + local MAPF for deadlock resolution

Auction-Based

CBF + auction prioritization for congested scenarios

Right-Hand Rule

Clockwise yielding with barrier-function safety constraints

Taxonomy of SMG Solvers

Desirable Properties of SMG Solvers

🛡️
Safety
Avoid collisions at all times. Achieved via ORCA, CBFs, or MPC with collision constraints. Critical in tight SMG spaces.
▶️
Liveness
Continuously make progress toward goals. Prevents deadlocks where overly-safe robots stall forever.
⚖️
Fairness
Ensure equitable outcomes. Measured by the gap between each agent's reward and the socially optimal allocation.
🤝
Social Compliance
Respect norms and expectations in shared spaces. Navigate without annoying or disturbing humans around.
📈
Scalability
Handle growing agent counts without performance loss. Evaluated on coupling set size, not total agents.

Evaluation Metrics

Beyond standard MRN metrics, SMGs require game-aware metrics that capture the unique challenges of high-agency interactions.

ΔV
Avg ΔV (Velocity Change)
Measures movement smoothness — how much an agent's speed changes on average per time step. Lower is smoother.
⌚
Average Delay
Additional travel time due to multi-agent interactions compared to solo navigation. Captures congestion impact.
↹
Path Deviation
Hausdorff distance between actual and ideal paths. Quantifies detours caused by interactions and deadlock avoidance.
⇄
Flow Rate
Density-normalized throughput at bottlenecks (doorways, corridors). Analogous to traffic flow per unit time per unit width.
⚖
Fairness (TTF)
Game-theoretic measure of how equitably rewards are distributed compared to the socially optimal cooperative strategy.
IS
Influence Score
Counterfactual measure of how much an agent's presence perturbs others' control behavior. Higher = more disruptive.

General Trends & Open Challenges

📷
Visual Inputs
Moving from perfect state to onboard sensors (LiDAR, cameras). Safe and deadlock-free MRN with high-dimensional inputs like point clouds remains a key challenge.
🧑
SMGs with Humans
Robots navigating among pedestrians in dense environments. Requires predicting human intent and being socially compliant while avoiding deadlocks.
🌎
Digital Twins
High-fidelity simulators reflecting real-world complexity. Current simulators lack multi-agent support in constrained indoor environments.
